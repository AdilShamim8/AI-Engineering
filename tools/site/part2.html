This repository is a free, self-study library for becoming an AI engineer, written from first principles so you can start from zero. It has three layers that work together: a technical curriculum of six numbered sections (101 LLM Literacy through 106 AI Security & Safety) that build on each other in learning order; roadmaps — guided day-by-day study plans through that same material, from a 286-session full roadmap to a 100-day accelerated plan; and interview & career material, including a 100-question bank where every question carries a worked expert answer. Inside section 105 sits a complete 19-lecture course on LLM evaluations — every lecture transcribed from Hindi to English, expanded into structured notes, and cross-linked to research-grade reference material. What it does not pretend to be: a paid bootcamp, a substitute for building things, or a source of unverified numbers — the evidence section keeps the discipline at the front door.

§01 · Beginner onboarding

New here? Start with this section

The repository has three layers. You don't need all of them at once — pick the layer that matches your situation, and open the others when you need them.

Layer 1 · The knowledge

Technical curriculum, 101–106. Six numbered sections — LLM Literacy → Application Development → Agentic AI → LLMOps → Evaluation → Security & Safety — written from first principles: no machine-learning background required. The curriculum ↓

Layer 2 · The schedule

Roadmaps. Guided, day-by-day study plans through the same material: a 286-session full roadmap, a 100-day accelerated plan, 100 interview questions, and 12 portfolio projects. The roadmaps ↓

Layer 3 · The job

Interview & career material. The AI-engineer role explained, learning paths from five adjacent careers, a portfolio guide, and interview preparation where every question carries a worked answer. Interview prep ↓

Prerequisites — you need less than you think You can already program (Python is the most useful language here). That's it. No machine-learning background is required — section 101 teaches every concept from scratch, and every later section links back to what it builds on.

Your first 60 minutes — a concrete first session
Read the repository's own introduction~10 min

Open the main README and skim how the three layers fit together. Don't follow every link yet — just get the map.

Read your very first technical note~15 min

"What Is a Large Language Model?" — the conceptual layer, explained from zero. This is note 1 of the whole curriculum; if you understand it, you're ready for everything that follows.

See where you're headed~10 min

Skim Module 1 of the Accelerated Roadmap (LLM fundamentals, days 1–15). You won't know these terms yet — that's the point: it's a preview of the trail, not today's climb.

Locate your personal entry point~25 min

If you already work in tech, read the transition guide for your current role in Learning Paths — data engineer, data scientist, ML engineer, backend, frontend, each with a duration estimate. Coming from outside tech? Follow the Full Roadmap, which starts at software fundamentals.

Roadmaps versus curriculum — how they fit together

Roadmaps = the guided tour

They tell you what to study and in what order — your schedule. The full roadmap covers 100% of the field in 16 levels; the accelerated one compresses it to the core ~80% in 100 days. Follow a roadmap as your plan.

Curriculum = the reference library

Deep notes on each topic, organized 101–106. Whenever a roadmap mentions a topic you want in full, open the matching curriculum section. Use them as your dictionary while you build.
